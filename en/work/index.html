01
Context

Finding the gap between test coverage and product risk

A complex software product already had a substantial automated testing baseline, but important workflows continued to experience regressions.

The question was not whether tests existed. It was whether existing protection matched the areas where product risk was materializing.
02
Diagnostic

Evidence correlation

Sources were not interpreted independently. They were correlated to locate where existing protection did not match observed product risk.

KEY FINDING

The primary gap was not simply a lack of automated tests.

Existing coverage was strongest around isolated behavior, while recurring risk concentrated in workflows spanning multiple screens, states and user roles.

03
Engineering decision

Preserve useful lower-level protection, avoid arbitrary coverage targets, prioritize critical workflows and strengthen the E2E layer where the evidence showed a gap.

Before scaling coverage, strengthen the framework so additional tests do not multiply flakiness and maintenance cost.

Preserve useful lower-level tests→Prioritize material workflows→Harden the E2E foundation→Expand targeted protection
04
E2E implementation

Building the regression safety net the diagnostic called for

The E2E foundation was strengthened before coverage expanded: typed support, reusable commands, stable selectors, deterministic data ownership, session handling and clearer execution conventions.
